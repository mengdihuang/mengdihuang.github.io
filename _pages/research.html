Research Summary

My doctoral research, advised by Drs. Wendi Gardner and Daniel Molden, examined well-being from two angles: how self-concept shapes decision-making, and how people seek support and navigate conflict within their social networks. Before Northwestern, I worked with Dr. Fan Yang at the University of Chicago on how people evaluate what constitutes a good life. I received my B.S. from UCSD, where I was part of the Empathy and Emotion Lab.

Selected Publications

The associations between self-compassion and adult attachment: A meta-analysis

Self-compassion is thought to grow out of secure caregiving, so we tested how strongly it relates to adult attachment. Synthesizing 46 studies (N = 17,650), we found robust negative correlations between self-compassion and both attachment anxiety (r = −.42) and avoidance (r = −.32). All six components of self-compassion were linked to attachment insecurity, and age moderated the link between avoidance and self-compassion.

Self-Transcendence or Self-Enhancement: People's Perceptions of Meaning and Happiness in Relation to the Self

What makes an experience feel meaningful rather than just happy? Across 7 preregistered studies (N = 1,362), we compared how much people weigh benefits to society (self-transcendence) versus benefits to the self (self-enhancement). Judgments of meaning consistently relied more on benefits to society than judgments of happiness did, whether people were evaluating jobs, other people's lives, advice, or their own experiences, though self-benefits mattered somewhat more when people judged their own lives.
